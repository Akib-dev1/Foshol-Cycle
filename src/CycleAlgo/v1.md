# Fosol-Cycle v1 — API-Backed Crop Rotation Explorer

> **Status:** Implementation specification for the agreed v1 approach. Agricultural thresholds, rotation restrictions, preparation times, and economic assumptions still require sourced local evidence and review.
>
> **Pilot:** Rajshahi, Bangladesh. The interface displays all 64 districts, but recommendations are enabled only where adequate local evidence exists.
>
> **Purpose:** Help users explore three-crop sequences and compare their timing, resource requirements, potential financial returns, and environmental exposure. This is decision support, not a field prescription or a guarantee.

## 1. Product scope

The website has two main pages:

1. **District map:** Select a district from a Bangladesh map or an accessible district dropdown.
2. **District explorer:** View eligible first crops, choose one, explore the next two crops, and compare complete branches.

Each complete branch contains exactly:

```text
First crop → Second crop → Third crop
```

The explorer assumes planting can begin on the displayed starting date. It does not ask whether the user's field is ready or assess an existing standing crop.

Display this assumption clearly:

> Options assume planting can begin on the date shown. Local field conditions and management still need checking.

The initial date defaults to the current date in Bangladesh. It may be changed through a simple date control. All calculations use the displayed date, not an undisclosed server date.

### In scope

- NASA API-backed environmental observations and historical records.
- Local crop profiles and planting calendars.
- Three common filters: season/climate, soil/land, and water compatibility.
- Calendar-based expansion into two subsequent crops.
- Historical seasonal outlooks for future growing periods.
- Explicit preparation and waiting gaps.
- Branch comparisons for time, costs, resources, potential returns, and diversity.
- Clear conditions, sources, missing evidence, and uncertainty.

### Not in scope

- Checking whether the user's current field is available.
- Generating recommendations for unsupported districts.
- Exact future weather, yield, or profit predictions.
- A physical simulation of soil nutrients or soil-health changes.
- A universal numerical suitability score.
- An LLM making or overriding agricultural decisions.
- Expanding beyond the two crops after the selected first crop.

## 2. Data sources and their responsibilities

| Source | Role | Must not be assumed to provide |
|---|---|---|
| NASA POWER APIs | Available historical/recent temperature, rainfall, and other supported environmental variables. | A future weather forecast, crop calendars, irrigation access, or market prices. |
| NASA SMAP data access services | Soil-moisture observations or estimates, depending on the selected product. | Field pH, nutrients, texture, drainage, irrigation access, or future moisture after a chosen crop. |
| Local crop dataset | Crop identity, variety, planting windows, duration ranges, environmental guidance, family, legume status, and sources. | Validated financial estimates or complete rotation rules unless explicitly supplied. |
| Local soil/land evidence | Available pH, texture, drainage, salinity, and geographic suitability information. | Uniform conditions across every field in a district. |
| Local agricultural/economic evidence | Preparation time, sequence restrictions, management requirements, yields, production costs, and farm-gate prices. | Guaranteed outcomes for an individual farmer. |

### Existing repository assets

The files directly inside `Data/` include eight researched crop profiles and two NASA POWER CSV exports. These are useful for understanding fields, testing parsers, and checking results, but the production environmental flow must use NASA APIs.

The existing POWER files include `GWETTOP`, which is MERRA-2 surface soil wetness. It must not be labeled SMAP.

There is no SMAP file directly inside `Data/` at the time of this specification. A specific SMAP product and access route must be selected and verified before implementing that integration.

**Do not read, modify, regenerate, or otherwise use `Data/DataNEVERBEUSED/` for this implementation.**

The current crop profiles do not include tomato, cucumber, or garlic. Those crops must not appear as evaluated options until researched profiles are added.

### API integration requirements

Before implementation, record for each selected product:

- Product name and version.
- Actual endpoint or documented access mechanism.
- Authentication requirements and usage limits.
- Variables, units, valid ranges, and missing-value conventions.
- Spatial resolution, temporal resolution, coverage, and publication latency.
- Quality flags and relevant limitations.
- Method used to associate observations with the selected district.

Do not assume SMAP has the same point-query interface as POWER. Use the documented access method for the chosen product. Keep credentials server-side where required.

## 3. District support and geographic interpretation

Use a stable district ID to connect the map, local evidence, environmental queries, and results.

For the pilot, a documented representative location may be used for environmental queries. If so, say that the result represents that location/grid rather than a measured district average.

Do not describe a single point as an area average. If district aggregation is implemented, document which cells are included, quality filtering, and aggregation method.

For an unsupported district, display:

> Local crop evidence is not available for this district yet.

Do not silently substitute Rajshahi crop rules or environmental data.

## 4. End-to-end user flow

```text
Open website
    ↓
Select district
    ↓
Load local crop evidence and cached/API environmental data
    ↓
Evaluate first crops for the displayed planting date
    ↓
Show candidate, conditional, and unsupported options with reasons
    ↓
User selects a first crop
    ↓
Estimate harvest and preparation ranges
    ↓
Evaluate second-crop options at the next planting opportunity
    ↓
Evaluate third-crop options after each selected second crop
    ↓
Compare complete three-crop branches by user preference
```

The UI expands the tree progressively. With eight crop profiles, evaluating complete paths after first-crop selection is small enough to do directly; no optimization framework is necessary. The UI need not render all paths at once.

## 5. Common evaluation outcomes

Each individual check returns one of:

| Check status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `PASS` | Available evidence supports this check under the stated assumptions. |
| `CAUTION` | A known concern or prerequisite needs attention. |
| `UNKNOWN` | Required evidence is missing, unsuitable, or insufficient. |
| `UNSUPPORTED` | A documented rule conflicts with this planting opportunity or scenario. |

Overall crop/path presentation:

- **Candidate:** Required checks pass without a known blocking conflict. This is not a guarantee of suitability.
- **Conditional:** No established blocking conflict, but at least one relevant caution or unknown remains.
- **Not supported:** At least one documented blocking conflict exists. Explain the conflict.

Unknown values never become an automatic pass. They also do not automatically prove that a crop cannot grow.

Keep excluded options inspectable through a small “Why were other crops not shown?” section rather than silently dropping them.

## 6. Filter 1 — Season and climate fit

### 6.1 Calendar eligibility

Compare a candidate's proposed planting date with its sourced windows:

1. Inside the preferred window: preferred calendar fit.
2. After the preferred window but inside a documented supported-late window: conditional late fit.
3. Beyond documented planting support: outside support for this opportunity.
4. Missing or geographically inappropriate window evidence: unknown.

If a preferred window has ended and no late support is documented, state that planting is outside the documented window. Do not call the preferred endpoint a proven biological deadline.

For the first-crop list, distinguish **plant now** from **plant after waiting**. A future-window crop is not a plant-now option; if shown, display its earliest planting date and apply the configured maximum waiting rule.

Attach the correct year to every month/day window. Handle windows crossing New Year, such as Boro. Never advance to a distant future season silently just to make a crop appear eligible.

### 6.2 Climate exposure

Compare the proposed growing period with available environmental evidence. Prefer locally applicable, stage-specific warning rules when available.

If only generalized temperature ranges exist, present the calculation as a general exposure indicator. Do not interpret it as a validated crop-failure model or automatically apply a mean-temperature range to daily maxima/minima.

Rainfall totals, dry spells, and heavy-rain events may add context where their definitions and crop relevance are supported. Generalized precipitation requirement fields must not be treated as daily thresholds or seasonal water requirements without checking their definitions.

Weather warnings usually create cautions. Hard rejection requires a documented rule appropriate to the variable, crop, stage, location, and time scale.

## 7. Filter 2 — Soil and land fit

Evaluate only properties for which suitable local evidence exists:

- pH.
- Texture.
- Drainage/land condition.
- Salinity.
- Other explicitly sourced geographic restrictions.

District-level evidence is regional context, not an individual field measurement. Preserve ranges and geographic variation instead of inventing one field value.

Use SMAP moisture as environmental context at the product's actual resolution. Do not use an unvalidated moisture threshold to declare that land is ready, waterlogged, or unsuitable for a particular crop.

If pH, texture, or drainage is unavailable, return `UNKNOWN` for the corresponding check. Do not infer these properties from SMAP or POWER wetness.

## 8. Filter 3 — Water compatibility

Use crop irrigation guidance and known water-access information.

An optional simple user control may be provided:

```text
Water access: Rainfed / Irrigation available / Not sure
```

Without user input, default to **Not sure**, not irrigation available.

Examples:

- An irrigation-dependent crop with unknown access: conditional, “Irrigation confirmation required.”
- An irrigation-dependent crop under a rainfed scenario: apply sourced compatibility guidance; do not assume rainfall will satisfy its requirements.
- Irrigation available: prerequisite partly addressed, not proof that the amount, timing, reliability, or cost is adequate.

Do not calculate exact irrigation demand as generic crop water need minus POWER rainfall. Effective rainfall, evapotranspiration, soil storage, and management are also relevant.

Missing water-demand evidence must remain unknown. It must not receive a low-demand label or a favorable resource ranking.

## 9. Seasonal outlook — the v1 predictive component

### 9.1 Method: historical seasonal replay

For a proposed growing schedule:

1. Select a documented historical analysis period.
2. Map the proposed crop schedule to corresponding periods in historical years.
3. Retain seasons with adequate valid observations for the indicator being calculated.
4. Calculate typical conditions and variability across those seasons.
5. Count how often a defined warning condition occurred.
6. Return the indicator definition, sample count, period, and missing coverage with the result.

Example output wording:

> This schedule encountered the defined heat-warning condition in X of Y evaluated historical seasons.

`X` and `Y` must come from actual calculations. This is historical exposure frequency, not the probability that the crop will fail.

### 9.2 Time handling

- Preserve the seasonal calendar when mapping schedules across years.
- Handle cross-year crops, leap days, and duration ranges explicitly.
- Do not treat an incomplete current year as a complete historical season.
- Assess coverage for the actual crop period, not just whether a year appears in the API response.
- Use a documented coverage requirement; record excluded seasons and why they were excluded.

### 9.3 Recent versus future conditions

Recent observations describe the present starting context. Historical seasonal replay describes plausible seasonal variability for future branches.

Do not carry today's moisture reading unchanged through all future crops. Do not claim that the selected crop's effects on future soil moisture or nutrients have been simulated.

Label the section:

> Seasonal outlook — based on historical conditions, not a weather forecast.

A separate near-term forecast integration can be added later. Its source, forecast horizon, and uncertainty must remain distinct from historical NASA observations.

POWER and SMAP may have different historical coverage. Use valid coverage separately for each indicator. A comparison requiring both must use a compatible overlapping period; do not fabricate older SMAP observations.

## 10. Timeline calculations and branch expansion

### 10.1 Duration reference point

Use the selected variety's verified duration range. Confirm whether it is measured from sowing, transplanting, or another establishment stage.

For transplanted crops, do not automatically add nursery age to a duration that already includes it, or assume that total variety duration is time occupying the main field. Resolve the definition from the source; if unresolved, mark timing conditional.

### 10.2 Harvest and preparation

For a planting-date range:

```text
Earliest harvest = earliest planting + minimum verified duration
Latest harvest   = latest planting + maximum verified duration

Earliest ready = earliest harvest + minimum preparation time
Latest ready   = latest harvest + maximum preparation time
```

Preparation includes applicable residue handling, drainage/drying, harvesting logistics, and land preparation. Do not double-count operations already included in the duration definition.

The current crop CSV does not validate preparation times. Obtain local evidence or use a clearly labeled prototype scenario range. If neither is available, timing-dependent suitability remains unknown.

### 10.3 Next planting opportunity

For each applicable next-crop window:

```text
Earliest next planting = max(earliest ready, window opening)
Latest next planting   = max(latest ready, window opening)
```

Check both dates against preferred and supported-late bounds. Use the latest plausible date for a conservative main label while retaining the earliest date in the explanation.

If the range straddles a boundary, expose that uncertainty. Do not hide it by averaging dates.

If a planting range propagates into the next crop, keep that range in subsequent harvest calculations. For climate analysis, account for alternative dates in the range or explicitly label the selected scenario date; do not imply that one chosen date represents every possible timing outcome.

### 10.4 Waiting and maximum gaps

Distinguish:

- **Preparation interval:** Harvest to field readiness.
- **Waiting interval:** Field readiness to the next crop's planting opportunity.
- **Total between-crop interval:** Preparation plus waiting.

For each scenario/date realization:

```text
Waiting interval = next planting date − field-ready date
```

Calculate the range consistently; do not subtract unrelated endpoints and label the result a reliable bound.

Apply a configurable maximum waiting interval. No numeric value is established by this specification.

Every limit must state its basis:

1. A sourced crop-transition/agronomic constraint, or
2. A product relevance limit used to avoid presenting long waits.

If only some timing realizations exceed the limit, mark the branch conditional. If the entire branch exceeds a product limit, exclude it from normal results with:

> Exceeds this explorer's maximum waiting gap.

Do not label a product cutoff a scientifically safe maximum or claim that longer fallow is universally impossible. Waiting/fallow is not automatically unsafe or undesirable.

### 10.5 Depth and continuity

Expand only until the path contains three crops. Include actual dates and gaps even when the sequence crosses calendar years. No fixed one-year completion limit is imposed in v1.

Evaluate every transition in the three-crop path. Do not claim that it is a validated indefinitely repeatable rotation: the third-to-first transition is not assessed in this scope.

## 11. Rotation compatibility for second and third crops

Apply the same common filters to every crop node, then add sequence checks:

- Documented crop-to-crop restrictions.
- Repeated families and previous occurrences within the three-crop path.
- Legume presence.
- Management prerequisites tied to the preceding crop, where sourced.

Known validated sequence restrictions may block a branch. Same-family repetition alone is a transparent diversity indicator, not automatic rejection unless local evidence supports that restriction.

Family changes do not establish disease control, soil-carbon improvement, or nitrogen balance. Legume presence indicates potential biological nitrogen fixation under suitable conditions, not a guaranteed fertilizer saving.

## 12. Comparing complete branches

### 12.1 Completion time

```text
Branch completion time = third harvest date − first planting date
```

Show a range. Display preparation and waiting gaps separately in the timeline.

Rank by a clearly stated convention, such as the latest estimated completion date, and disclose overlapping ranges. Do not claim a definitive timing winner where uncertainty makes the distinction unclear.

### 12.2 Resources

Keep these separate:

- Estimated cash costs.
- Irrigation requirements/dependence.
- Labor requirements and relevant peak periods.
- Equipment or special-management prerequisites.

Do not combine unlike units into one unexplained resource score. A low-cash branch may require more labor or reliable irrigation.

Aggregate only comparable quantities. Annual total labor, for example, does not establish whether peak labor can be supplied on time. Qualitative irrigation guidance must not be summed as if it were a measured volume.

### 12.3 Economic estimates

Use a common comparison area, initially **per hectare**, with a clearly stated currency, initially **BDT**.

For crop `i`:

```text
Estimated gross revenue_i = marketable yield_i × farm-gate price_i
Estimated net return_i    = estimated gross revenue_i − production cost_i

Branch gross revenue = sum(crop gross revenues)
Branch net return    = branch gross revenue − total branch costs
```

Use compatible units, such as tonnes/hectare with BDT/tonne. Include preparation/transition costs once, not zero times or twice.

Record:

- Yield source, production conditions, and whether yield is attainable, trial, or typical farm yield.
- Price location, grade, farm-gate/wholesale distinction, date, and source.
- Cost components and date, including whether labor, irrigation, land rental, transport, and other material items are included.
- Whether the return excludes important costs. If so, label it accordingly rather than calling it full profit.

Present low/base/high scenarios only when their assumptions are explicit. These are scenarios, not statistical confidence intervals. Do not silently assume all crops receive optimal yield and the highest price.

The existing reference yields are insufficient by themselves to establish expected farm profit. NASA APIs do not supply the missing economic evidence.

If an essential input is missing, return **Economic estimate unavailable**. Partial cost totals must not win a lowest-cost comparison.

### 12.4 Total cost versus upfront cash

Total production cost is not the same as upfront cash required. A branch may use proceeds from an earlier harvest to finance a later crop.

Only display **Lowest upfront cash** when there is a sourced cash-flow schedule for expenses and receipts. Otherwise use **Lowest estimated total cost** and say that financing needs have not been modeled.

### 12.5 Different completion horizons

Three-crop branches may finish at different times. Display total return and completion time together.

Highest total return is not automatically the best use of land over time. V1 need not annualize returns; if it does later, the assumptions about subsequent land use, repetition, and seasonal feasibility must be stated.

## 13. Preference-based branch suggestions

Offer simple preference controls:

- **Fastest completion**.
- **Lowest estimated total cost**.
- **Least irrigation dependence**, where comparable evidence supports the distinction.
- **Highest estimated net return**.
- **More crop diversity**.
- **Lowest upfront cash**, only when cash-flow evidence exists.

Use the relevant explicit metric to order comparable branches. No weighted overall score is required.

For diversity, show family count and legume presence separately. A simple family-count ordering is a product convention, not a measured soil-health ranking.

Rules:

1. Known blocking constraints cannot be overridden by preferences.
2. Show candidate and conditional paths in separate groups.
3. A path with missing evidence for the chosen metric is unranked for that metric, not assigned a favorable default.
4. Keep ties and overlapping estimates visible.
5. Show why the highlighted path matches the preference and what trade-off it brings.
6. If no path can be supported or compared, say so. Never force a winner.

Suggested badges:

```text
Fastest estimated completion
Lowest estimated total cost
Highest estimated net return
More crop-family diversity
Irrigation confirmation required
```

Every superlative means **among the evaluated, comparable branches**, not all possible agricultural choices.

## 14. Minimal implementation structure

Use the existing project's language and patterns. Do not introduce a database, job queue, optimization framework, or machine-learning dependency just for v1.

The minimum flow is:

```text
Local crop/soil/economic evidence
                 +
Cached NASA API observations and history
                 ↓
Normalized district environmental context
                 ↓
One shared candidate/transition evaluator
                 ↓
Three-crop path expansion
                 ↓
Metric calculations and preference sorting
                 ↓
District explorer UI
```

### Shared evaluator inputs

- District and geographic evidence scope.
- Crop profile and proposed planting-date range.
- Previous crops in the path, if any.
- Environmental context for the relevant period.
- Local soil/land evidence.
- Optional water-access scenario.
- Preparation and waiting rules with their sources/assumptions.

### Shared evaluator outputs

- Crop and variety identity.
- Planting and harvest ranges.
- Preparation and waiting ranges.
- Individual check statuses, reasons, and evidence references.
- Historical outlook indicators and data coverage.
- Preconditions and missing evidence.
- Overall presentation status.

### Branch outputs

- Three evaluated crop nodes and their transitions.
- Completion-time range.
- Comparable resource metrics.
- Economic scenarios or explicit unavailable status.
- Diversity indicators.
- Relevant preference ordering and explanations.
- Data timestamps and assumptions.

Use one evaluator for every crop. Crop differences belong in sourced data, not separate hard-coded functions for rice, wheat, lentil, and so on.

### 14.1 Algorithm-first handoff and JSON contract

The algorithm is built first. The frontend teammate builds around its documented request/response contract. The algorithm must therefore return structured, JSON-serializable results rather than HTML, chart markup, or explanation text alone.

**A JSON response is normally returned directly from an API request. Do not create a new JSON file on disk for every user click.** A saved example JSON file is a development/handoff artifact, not the runtime transport.

Implement the decision logic as a callable function accepting a request object and returning a result object. Keep HTTP handling as a thin wrapper so the same logic can be checked without running a web server. Use the existing implementation language; the contract does not require Python, Node, or a particular framework.

### 14.2 Minimal request contract

Use one endpoint initially:

```http
POST /api/explore
Content-Type: application/json
```

Initial district exploration request:

```json
{
  "district_id": "rajshahi",
  "start_date": "2026-11-06",
  "first_crop_id": null,
  "water_access": "unknown",
  "preference": "fastest_completion"
}
```

After the user selects a first crop, send the same request with that crop's actual ID. Return complete three-crop paths rooted in that crop. The frontend can progressively expand second/third crops by grouping the returned paths; it does not need a separate algorithm endpoint for each tree node.

Request rules:

| Field | Contract |
|---|---|
| `district_id` | Required stable district ID. Do not use a translated display name as the key. |
| `start_date` | Required valid `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar date, interpreted as a Bangladesh local agricultural date. |
| `first_crop_id` | `null` for first-crop options; otherwise an existing crop-profile ID. |
| `water_access` | `rainfed`, `irrigation_available`, or `unknown`; default `unknown`. |
| `preference` | `fastest_completion`, `lowest_total_cost`, `least_irrigation_dependence`, `highest_net_return`, or `more_crop_diversity`. Default `fastest_completion`. |

Add `lowest_upfront_cash` only when the cash-flow evidence and calculation described in Section 12.4 are implemented. Reject invalid enum values rather than silently substituting a different preference.

The engine must re-evaluate a supplied crop ID under the request conditions. A frontend selection is not evidence that a crop passes the filters.

### 14.3 Stable response envelope

Every successful calculation returns these top-level fields:

| Field | Shape and meaning |
|---|---|
| `schema_version` | String, initially `"1"`. Change for incompatible contract changes. |
| `generated_at` | UTC ISO timestamp for this response. |
| `request` | Validated effective input, including applied defaults. |
| `status` | `ok`, `partial`, `no_supported_options`, `unsupported_district`, or `data_unavailable`. |
| `district` | Object containing district ID, display name, and geographic interpretation. |
| `assumptions` | Array of explicit scenario/product assumptions. |
| `data_sources` | Array of source objects with stable references, product/version where applicable, geographic scope, observation coverage, retrieval time, and freshness/quality notes. |
| `crop_options` | Array of evaluated first-crop options, including unsupported options with reasons. |
| `branches` | Array of evaluated complete three-crop paths; empty before a first crop is selected or when no path is supported. |
| `ranking` | Object containing preference, ranked branch IDs, unranked branch IDs, method, and tie/uncertainty notes. |
| `warnings` | Array of objects containing stable `code`, readable `message`, and relevant source/crop/branch references where applicable. |

Status interpretation:

- `ok`: Requested calculation completed without material missing-data limitations; does not mean agricultural certainty.
- `partial`: Useful results exist, but material missing evidence or provider limitations affect them.
- `no_supported_options`: Calculation completed, but no option/path meets the stated rules for this request.
- `unsupported_district`: Local evidence is not available for the selected district.
- `data_unavailable`: Required environmental inputs are unavailable and no useful assessment can be made.

Use a documented precedence when more than one condition applies: unsupported district first, then no useful data, then no supported options, then partial, otherwise ok. Preserve all relevant warnings regardless of the main status.

These outcome statuses describe a valid calculation request and can use HTTP 200. Use HTTP 400 for invalid input and HTTP 500 for unexpected internal failures. Do not disguise an internal bug as an ordinary no-options result.

Error response shape:

```json
{
  "schema_version": "1",
  "error": {
    "code": "INVALID_REQUEST",
    "message": "start_date must be a valid YYYY-MM-DD date.",
    "field": "start_date"
  }
}
```

Never return credentials, raw stack traces, or secret provider headers to the frontend.

### 14.4 Crop, branch, and metric objects

Every crop option contains:

- `crop_id`, `name_en`, `name_bn`, and reference variety.
- Overall `status`: `candidate`, `conditional`, or `unsupported`.
- `checks`: objects keyed by `calendar`, `climate`, `soil`, `water`, and `rotation` where applicable.
- Each check contains `status` (`pass`, `caution`, `unknown`, or `unsupported`), stable `code`, readable `reason`, and `source_ids`.
- Explicit planting/harvest ranges, or `null` plus an explanation if unavailable.
- Reasons, cautions, missing-evidence identifiers, and seasonal-outlook indicators.

Use lowercase machine statuses in JSON. They correspond to the uppercase check vocabulary in Section 5; UI labels may be translated without changing the codes.

Every returned branch contains:

- A deterministic `branch_id`, unique for the selected request and ordered crop/window choices.
- `status`: `candidate` or `conditional`; blocked paths belong in exclusion explanations, not normal branch rankings.
- `crops`: an ordered array of exactly three fully evaluated crop nodes, with positions 1–3.
- `transitions`: an ordered array of two objects describing preparation, waiting, applicable limits, checks, assumptions, and exclusions/cautions.
- `metrics`: completion days, production cost, net return, irrigation evidence, labor/equipment evidence, distinct family count, and legume presence.
- `reasons`, `cautions`, `missing_evidence`, and `source_ids`.

Date ranges use `{"earliest": "YYYY-MM-DD", "latest": "YYYY-MM-DD"}`. Numeric ranges use `{"min": number, "max": number}`. Unknown quantities use `null`, not zero, `NaN`, an empty string, or an invented neutral score.

Metric field names or attached metadata must identify units and basis. For example, `production_cost_bdt_per_ha` and `completion_days`. Economic scenario objects must identify their assumptions and included/excluded costs; do not replace a scenario with an unexplained single number.

Outlook indicators include their definition, evaluated period, sample size, valid coverage, and source references. If returning warning-season counts, include both numerator and denominator. No samples means unavailable, not zero risk.

Rankings reference branch IDs instead of duplicating whole branch objects. Return candidate and conditional ranking groups separately, with deterministic ordering for ties and a note that the tie-break is for display only. Unavailable metrics remain unranked.

### 14.5 Frontend ownership versus algorithm ownership

**Algorithm owns:** validation, data interpretation, agricultural checks, timeline calculations, branch eligibility, metrics, rankings, source references, and uncertainty explanations.

**Frontend owns:** map selection, controls, loading states, tree/card rendering, expanded-node state, display formatting, localization, and responsive/accessibility behavior.

The frontend must not independently calculate profit, infer pass/fail from colors, or repeat agricultural rules. Display the engine's structured results. It may locally reorder an already returned complete result only using the same documented ranking rules; a new request is the simplest initial implementation.

### 14.6 Required handoff artifacts

Deliver together:

1. Runnable algorithm code with a short command showing how to execute it.
2. A documented callable entry point and the HTTP endpoint if hosted separately.
3. The finalized JSON request/response contract, with every nested field documented. Keep this specification updated as concrete field shapes are finalized.
4. `example_request.json` and a matching **complete**, schema-consistent `example_response.json` from the implemented algorithm, including all three crops when a first crop is selected.
5. Small saved examples for important frontend states: initial options, conditional branches/missing economics, no supported paths, unsupported district, and invalid input.
6. Runnable checks proving responses serialize as valid JSON, required keys/statuses are consistent, referenced branch/source IDs exist, and full paths have exactly three crops and two transitions.
7. Environment/setup instructions naming required credentials without including secrets, supported districts, data limitations, and API startup configuration.

Before real calculation output exists, mock responses may unblock interface work, but label them explicitly as mock data. Never present invented values as observed NASA results or calculated recommendations.

No new schema framework is required just for the handoff. Use existing tooling or small contract checks; add a machine-readable schema when it prevents actual integration errors. The important requirement is one consistent, documented contract—not multiple conflicting example formats.

If frontend and algorithm are deployed on different origins, configure a bounded CORS allowlist for the actual frontend. Otherwise prefer same-origin routing and avoid unnecessary cross-origin setup.

## 15. API fetching, caching, and failures

- Fetch/cache environmental data by product, version, variables, location/area, date range, and relevant processing settings.
- Share the same cached history across branches that use it.
- Use refresh policies appropriate to each product's publication schedule; historical data does not need refetching on every click.
- Expose both observation dates and retrieval timestamps.
- Apply finite timeouts, bounded retries, and provider rate-limit handling.
- Validate provider responses before evaluating crops.
- Convert documented missing sentinels such as POWER `-999` into missing values, never physical observations.
- Respect quality flags and units.
- On an API failure, use an available cached response only with an explicit freshness warning, or mark the affected checks unavailable.
- Never replace missing moisture with zero or turn failed climate retrieval into a pass.
- Do not imply that SMAP, POWER, and local evidence have identical spatial resolution or dates.

The UI should remain usable when one provider fails. Show which parts of the comparison remain supported and which are unavailable.

## 17. Validation and runnable checks

Implementation must include a small runnable automated check covering the core evaluator and branch logic. Use the project's existing test tools if present; do not add a testing framework solely for this specification.

Minimum cases:

1. Preferred planting window passes at both boundaries.
2. A documented late planting date becomes conditional.
3. A date beyond documented support is not presented as a normal candidate.
4. Missing late-window support is not treated as a biological impossibility claim.
5. Cross-year windows and leap-year dates resolve correctly.
6. Duration ranges propagate rather than collapsing silently to an average.
7. Missing preparation evidence makes timing conditional/unknown.
8. Waiting limits distinguish partial range overlap from complete exclusion.
9. Missing soil or irrigation evidence does not automatically pass or reject a crop.
10. API missing values, poor quality, and failed requests cannot create favorable results.
11. Incomplete historical crop periods are excluded according to the declared coverage rule.
12. A branch stops at exactly three crops.
13. Unsupported districts do not inherit Rajshahi results.
14. Preference changes cannot revive blocked branches.
15. Missing economic inputs cannot win cost or return rankings.
16. Financial units are converted correctly and transition costs are counted once.
17. No supported path returns an honest empty/insufficient-evidence result.

Use deterministic saved API response samples for automated checks so they do not depend on internet availability. These are test samples, not a replacement for the production API-backed flow. Keep them outside the prohibited data subfolder.

Software checks validate the calculations, not the agricultural truth of the rules. Local expert/source review remains a separate requirement.

## 19. Decisions that must be resolved before release

- Which SMAP product and documented access route will be used?
- Will environmental data represent a point/grid or a district aggregation?
- Which historical period and coverage requirement apply to each indicator?
- Which local soil/land evidence is available at useful resolution?
- Are crop durations measured from the establishment stage used by the engine?
- What preparation ranges are supported, and which remain assumptions?
- What maximum waiting limit will the product use, and how will it be labeled?
- Which crop-sequence restrictions are actually supported?
- Which comparable management, cost, yield, and farm-gate price evidence can support rankings?

These are evidence/configuration decisions, not reasons to build a more complicated algorithm. Unresolved items must remain visible in results.

## 20. Acceptance criteria

V1 is ready for demonstration when a user can:

1. Select Rajshahi from the Bangladesh map or district list.
2. Understand the planting-date assumption and environmental data freshness.
3. See why first crops are candidates, conditional, or outside support.
4. Choose a first crop and explore two subsequent crops with actual timeline ranges.
5. See preparation, waiting, and excluded-path explanations.
6. Compare complete branches by supported time/resource/economic/diversity metrics.
7. Select a preference and understand why a branch is highlighted.
8. Identify missing evidence and distinguish historical outlooks from forecasts or guarantees.

**Core rule: use APIs for environmental evidence, local research for agricultural and economic rules, one shared evaluator for every crop, and transparent metrics for choosing among three-crop branches.**
